Travel diary: the problems, the reverse engineering, the wire we kept

The earlier notes are dated waypoints. This one is the journey retold in order: what blocked us, how we attacked it, and which technical particulars survived into iCloudKit Extractor. It is still evidence—only arranged the way we actually lived it, with a colleague under Forensis-Lab, often in spare hours, from an ADP backup we could not buy off the shelf.

Departure: ADP, and learning to watch someone else’s binary

Late March 2026. Commercial suites failed on an ADP-protected iCloud backup. The operational question (“how do we get the data?”) collapsed into a research one: what does the iCloud section of Settings actually say on the wire?

We started with two observation channels we already held: a jailbroken iPhone and a licensed Elcomsoft Phone Breaker. Studying EPB was not “copy the GUI.” It was reverse engineering a protected Windows stack. That meant confronting commercial obfuscation—especially Themida-class packing: virtualized regions, API wrapping, anti-debug noise. We did not invent that discipline from scratch. We leaned on public research, including a student thesis line of work on unpacking / reconstructing Themida-protected executables (DBI-style write-then-execute detection, import recovery, PE rebuild thinking), and then applied those habits with x64dbg, memory dumps, and patient comparison of live sessions. The goal was never a cracked product; it was a map of requests and responses we could later reproduce ourselves.

Parallel to that, Frida on the phone and the browser surface taught the first authentication geography. iCloud.com gave idmsa web tokens—useful for Photos metadata and trusted-device / SMS flows, wrong for native mobile clients. GrandSlam / GsService2 on the device side was the real gate: lookup → init with A2k, u, cpd, ps: [s2k, s2k_fo] → complete with M1 only (an extra M2 yields ec=-22410). Anisette headers (X-Apple-I-MD, X-Apple-I-MD-M, RINFO, device id, client time) feed CPD; reminting machine data mid-SRP breaks the pair. Default iterations when the server omits i: 20016. Windows vs iOS RINFO constants in our builders (84215040 / 17106176) are still there because wrong identity fails quietly upstream of every CloudKit call.

First CloudKit names that stuck: container com.apple.backup.ios, bundle com.apple.backupd, zones _defaultZone and mbksync—not a vague “MobileSync” label. EPB was broken for our flows that month; Elcomsoft fixed later. We did not wait.

April: snapshots without the mountain

Mid-April we could list snapshots—records under S: after D:<sha>—while the IDE and the coding agent kept reshaping the tree around us. Elcomsoft shipped an iOS 26-capable build; with ADP still on it returned HTTP 403. That 403 was evidence: ADP is a server/crypto gate, not “old iOS.” We used their release as an oracle for how snapshot access had been unblocked at all, messy captures included.

What we still could not open was backupProperties—the Lockdown UDID and friends. Months later the path is ShareProtection (zone / one-shot catalog unwrap) → CUWK label backupProperties → PCSFP-GCM with AAD type0 = field name. Without a PCS KeySet on the session the field stays encrypted. That KeySet is an Octagon story; in April we only knew we were staring at ciphertext.

The Lock wall: Prepare Restore as a state machine

Listing is not restoring. On the phone you see a preparing phase; on the wire it is CloudKit ending in a Lock on mbksync before the large post-Lock catalog (N: / EN) opens. Diary shorthand “Prepare Restore Session” is that ordered climb. In IKE it is PrepareDownloadAsync / AcquireRestoreLockAsync—there is no separate public RPC named PrepareRestore in our tree.

The order we still refuse to reorder: zones → BackupAccount → D: → S: → (optional mobilebackup stub) → Lock → N:. Lock record: {deviceSha}:Lock, type Lock, owner = device SHA, duration 172800 s. Fail Foreground Restore hw/sha identity—or double-wrap a protobuf field name the wrong way (Lockb9 vs Lockb7)—and MobileBackupServerExtension answers Invalid lock id.

Help was an ugly coalition: Frida on the jailbroken phone, plus a few malformed EPB request/response pairs that were wrong for a clean download but perfect as fossils. Optional MBS hosts (application/vnd.com.apple.mbs+protobuf, X-Apple-MBS-Protocol-Version: 2.2) appeared as stubs or full-ish manifests.

Two timelines meet here, and it is easy to blur them. In April we could acquire Lock without yet decrypting backupProperties—the CloudKit restore host exam is separate from opening ADP fields. On the full path we ship today, escrow → PCS KeySet / zone masters should land before Lock and before any authorizeGet, or Ford / IKM work has no oxygen. That KeySet oxygen is the October Octagon climb (bottle PIN or Recovery Key), not something we already held on 29 April.

Into MMCS: two species of bytes on one host

Roughly a month of reverse engineering past Lock put us on 24 May 2026 into MMCS. The map changed from CloudKit records to edge-content hosts, ranged GETs, and blobs that refused to be one kind of thing. By then the phone and EPB were no longer daily crutches for the next problems; coding agents and older public fragments pointing at Octagon opened paths the commercial “normal restore” road never advertised. EPB stayed as an oracle for MMCS caching and chunk reassembly. Side-by-side through the summer, our reconstructions often held on the order of ~3000 more decrypted files—and still left a stubborn tail.

authorizeGet posts to {contentHost}/{dsid}/authorizeGet as application/vnd.com.apple.me.ubchunk+protobuf, with x-apple-mmcs-auth shaped as {hex21} {hex21} {CAog…}. Per-file entries carry assetSignature as f1 (typically 0x04‖20B), CAog as f2, companion as f4 (often 0x01‖20B). The response is FileGroups: one group ↔ one edge host, nested ChunkInfo (checksum 21 or 45 B, chunkEncryptionKey, ctLen, packOffset), trailing chunk references for assembly. Edge GETs use content-byte-range=start-end. Incomplete plans (missing URLs, range sum < declared size) are hard failures, not soft warnings.

Same host, different ranges: Ford key material versus data ChunkInfo. Mis-classify them and MAC checks fail with perfect confidence. That single sentence occupied most of June and July.

IKM, Ford, and the caching war

The file-list EncryptionKey looks like the key. It is the wrong key for Ford. Live behaviour—and later EPB/oracle traces—forced a narrow rule: IKM from contents.protectionInfo unwrap with the zone master, then HKDF-SHA256(ikm=fileKey, salt="PCSMMCS2", info="", len=64) (not the swapped salt/info orientation). Ford blob: lead ‖ nonce(16) ‖ tag(16) ‖ ct; AES-SIV with AD [nonce, lead]. Classic Auth Ford ctLen sits in [0x12, 65536]; larger bags need ByteRange length ≥ ctLen. Gate descriptors often carry 45-byte checksums led by 0C 0F 01 04.

Early instinct was to force the plan: guess windows, re-slice hosts, plant every lead-0x04 candidate. Those paths sometimes opened a file and also poisoned the checksum21-keyed .chunks/ cache with Ford-shaped prefixes where data should live. The structural fix was humbler and still in the code: treat MMCS like a store that can deliver chunk data after the Ford gate; SpillOpen only when size == ctLen, lead 0x04, and the blob opens exactly with IKM; never plant Ford without IKM; FillChunks for keyed storages first, then data; accept any lead byte for data CI21; detect Ford-prefix poison on data slices. Per-domain assembly sessions so FileGroup caches do not cross-contaminate.

One nibble: 0x04 versus 0xC4

August’s absurdity: we had trained ourselves that Ford CT at offset 0 starts with 0x04. Valid data chunks can start with 0xC4 (and other non-0x04 leads), including large bodies well over 64 KiB. Requiring 0x04 on data was a classifier bug, not an Apple gate. Gate45’s 0C0F0104 is descriptor metadata, not the data lead. Orphaned CloudKit names and third-party on-device crypto (Mozilla models in testing, and similar) sat outside the Ford/MMCS path entirely—another false trail until we named it as such.

Closing the loop on disk

By late August the protocol spine could feed an iTunes-shaped tree: Backup/{fileID[0..2]}/{fileID} with fileID as SHA1 hex (SHA1(domain + "-" + relativePath) in our Manifest rows), Manifest.db from decrypted MA: candidates, sidecars Info.plist / Status.plist / Manifest.plist (the last preferring decrypted backupProperties). Work lives under tmp/; .chunks die after success. Catalog evolution in tooling: modern S: → :CN: → EN:; classic S: → M: → files. Forensic tools finally saw a familiar backup—not because Apple documented it, but because we rebuilt the shape they already know how to open.

Octagon: the mountain behind the mountain

MMCS can fetch ciphertext forever; ADP still decides whether it is yours. October opened two doors into the circle.

Bottle / iCDP PIN. Escrowproxy get_records, labels com.apple.icdp.record.*. Recover is scarce against HSM (~10 attempts in lab notes)—irreversible in tooling. SRP secret is the device passcode, not legacy PCS dsid:dsid: x = H(salt ‖ H(dsid ":" passcode)), Basic(email, PET). EscrowBottle → BottledPeerEntropy (~72 B) → Cuttlefish fetchViableBottles on com.apple.security.keychain → peer keys → KeySet.

Recovery Key. When the PIN path is unavailable, the RK is not “another password sent to Apple.” Locally: HKDF-SHA384(ikm=UTF-8(RK), salt=UTF-8(altDSID), info, 56) with Recovery Signing Private Key / Recovery Encryption Private Key, deterministic P-384, PeerId RK-…. Then fetchRecoverableTLKShares for that peer, ECIES unwrap of tlkshare-…::RK-… records, OpenVault into Manatee / ProtectedCloudStorage / Passwords. Wrong RK: shares may list, unwrap count is zero. Correct RK: the same trust class as legitimate recovery—without pretending to be a hardware bottle. That is how backupProperties and zone masters finally stop being mythology.

What the journey taught us to leave unsettled

Private APIs are not a contract. Completeness is not every historical filename. Foreground HardwareId / plist SHA were stable across many captures for one analyzed account—not claimed universal. Emulated anisette ages; coherent ADI/akd (Frida or a maintained remote server) remains a prerequisite for GSA. The experimental phase note still holds: MIT, signed builds, Forensis-Lab attribution, no warranty that tomorrow’s wire matches today’s diary.

Looking back, the Themida / thesis homework mattered less as a single trick and more as a habit: treat protected software as a surface you instrument and compare, not as a temple. Frida, EPB fossils, authorizeGet FileGroups, Ford versus ChunkInfo, Octagon bottles and Recovery Key—each was a different mountain with the same method. The code is the packing list we kept.
